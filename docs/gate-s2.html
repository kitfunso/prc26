Ledger line

Board: N = 1 model submission (S1) plus 2 scored probes (P1, P2); 3 scored files per team per day is the cap, and the probe pair v8/v9 is scheduled for 2026-09-06 00:05 UTC. Holdout: Nh = 16 verdict lanes, 8 wins, 5 losses, 3 ties.

Multiplicity, stated in the noise yardstick. Each lane-A promotion since S1 was a k = 1 lane judged at bound 0 × SE. The paired SE per step is about 0.6 s (interval half-width 1.05 s divided by 1.96). Under a global null of no gain anywhere, sixteen one-sided 2.5% tests give 0.4 expected false wins and about a 33% chance of at least one. The section-3 rule does not depend on the chain position; but the 2 s floor does the work the alpha-spending does not: at SE 0.6 the floor sits 3.3 SE from zero, so a null step passes it with probability about 0.04%. Had the bound been charged for all sixteen lanes at once, E[max of 16 N(0,1)] × SE ≈ 1.77 × 0.6 ≈ 1.1 s, still under the +2.9 s observed on each step. The chain's weak point is not the null; it is that each step's true gain could be anywhere in [1.8, 3.9] (F10).

The rule, restated

A challenger wins when the paired bootstrap interval of its RMSE gain sits above zero and the point estimate clears the analytic optimism bound (E[max of k N(0,1)] × paired SE, k = the declared cell count) plus the 2 s floor. The interval crossing zero is a tie; a tie promotes the simpler model. Lane A decides promotion; lane B is judged on its own rows. No month feature (R7). Every lane is declared before it runs; an undeclared run is a diagnostic.

This verdict is only as good as this rule; attack the rule, not just the numbers.

The stricter reading (F10): require the whole interval above the floor. Under it H14 and H16 are ties, the tie promotes the simpler model, and M1 (the S1 file's lane-A model) stays champion. The rule as written decides on the point estimate. Which reading holds is your call, and it decides whether S2 is W1-A or a re-upload of M1.
