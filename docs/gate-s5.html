3. The decision rule, restated

A challenger replaces the champion when its paired improvement on the untouched holdout has a lower bound above zero AND a point estimate at or above 2.0 s, with an optimism bound added on top for the number of cells the lane searched. Ties promote the simpler model. The holdout is read once per lane and never to choose anything.

SB3+NN3 now meets that rule: lower bound +1.12, point estimate +2.07, and the bound that used to sit on top of it has been retired by measurement rather than by argument.

This verdict is only as good as this rule. Attack the rule, not just the numbers. The two places it is softest: 2.0 s is a constant chosen once and never derived, and it sits INSIDE the interval, so a true gain of 1.5 s would produce these rows about as readily as a true gain of 2.5 s.

4. Caution flags, all of them

  • The margin is 0.07 s. Nothing on this page makes it bigger.
  • The floor sits inside the interval [+1.12, +2.92], so these rows cannot rule out a true gain below the floor.
  • The board column is a projection off one controlled experiment. It has no interval, and every board projection this campaign made before that experiment was wrong by about 27%.
  • The blend weight was fitted against MC2 and is applied to SB3. It is not the best weight for SB3, and the holdout's own oracle says the gap is about 0.26 s.
  • The judged rows are two months of one year.
  • A banked 1.26 s of board, already paid for, has still never been applied to a submitted file.
