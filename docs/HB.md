Generated by `python -m taxi.hb`. A diagnostic. It declares no cell and decides no verdict.

## 1. The rows we are scored on are not the rows we measure

The ranking set holds 344,830 departures in January and July 2026, 5,287 of them in lane B; the holdout holds
344,419 in the same months of 2025, 5,373 in lane B. The ranking count is 11 short of the 344,841 rows the

cell mix and
the lane split. It never measured lane A, which is 98.5% of the rows, because it held the lane-A figure fixed while it moved
lane B. Both lanes below, each re-averaged under the ranking set's own airport-and-month counts, each with the worst holdout

## 2. The board file trains on months no holdout fit has seen

`taxi/s2.py` builds the upload from all twelve months of 2025. Every verdict in this campaign trains on ten, because January and
July are the holdout. `COLS_D1` carries no month, season or day-of-week column, so nothing tells either model which month it is in;
the only difference is which rows are present. That difference has never been priced. The COLUMN half of that sentence was
closed 2026-09-07 by `docs/SE.md`: a season column is worth +0.088 s in sample at month, +1.411 s at month, airport and hour,
and -0.042 s on the holdout in the only form the month-pair folds can actually validate, so what follows prices the only half that
moves anything. It is priceable without touching the holdout:
judge the even days of February and August, and let the arms differ only in whether the odd days of those months are in
training,
each arm holding the same row count so capacity is not the variable.

Judged rows: 162,854. Donor rows: 168,620.

| training rows | n | RMSE | February | August | vs A |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A judged months absent | 1,731,103 | 225.2866 s | 222.63 s | 227.35 s |  |
| B judged months swapped in | 1,731,103 | 221.3314 s | 214.13 s | 226.82 s | **+3.9552 s** [+1.19, +7.44] |
| C adjacent months swapped in | 1,731,103 | 224.9425 s | 222.08 s | 227.16 s | **+0.3441 s** [-0.15, +0.86] |
| D seasonal weights, no judged rows | 1,731,103 | 226.2310 s | 222.95 s | 228.77 s | **-0.9444 s** [-1.71, -0.19] |
| E judged rows and seasonal weights | 1,731,103 | 223.2734 s | 215.47 s | 229.21 s | **+2.0132 s** [-1.14, +5.85] |

The judged months' own rows are worth real seconds, and adjacency is not a substitute: arm C swaps in the two months either side
and lands worse than having no target rows at all. Weighting is worse still. Arm D leans the fit toward the season without any of
its rows and loses; arm E adds the weights on top of the rows that do help and gives back most of the gain. Only presence helps,
and it is not the season: nearly all of it is in February while August barely moves. Baseline 225.2866 s.

## 3. Weather decays in days; this does not

Section 2's donor rows are interleaved with the judged rows, one day apart, so they share weather fronts and the same week's
disruption. If that is all the gain is, it should fall away as the donors move back in time. It mostly does not. Judging the 22nd to
the 28th, with donors drawn from the week before or from the fortnight before that.

Judged rows: 78,489. Donors per arm: 78,686.

| training rows | n | RMSE | February | August | vs A |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A no judged-month rows | 1,731,103 | 236.1236 s | 254.94 s | 219.17 s |  |
| B donors 1 to 7 days earlier | 1,731,103 | 226.5494 s | 235.13 s | 219.12 s | **+9.5741 s** [+0.32, +21.77] |
| C donors 8 to 21 days earlier | 1,731,103 | 229.1923 s | 240.81 s | 219.02 s | **+6.9312 s** [+0.36, +15.77] |

Donors three weeks removed keep **72%** of what donors one week removed are worth, which is more than short-range weather
autocorrelation leaves. So this is not a weather effect that a better METAR join would capture. It is something slower that persists
across weeks inside one year, and it is again all in February: August gains nothing at any distance.

## 4. Does the same month a year earlier transfer?

Section 2 measured a month's own rows inside one year. The board's extra rows are not inside one year: the file trains on January
and July **2025** and is scored on January and July **2026**. Departures cannot answer this, because they carry one year of labels.
Arrivals can. The ranking file keeps a measured taxi-in on 2026 arrivals at the same ten airports, so a model trained on 2025
arrivals can be judged on 2026 ones, with the matching month a year earlier present or absent and the row count held equal.

| judged month, 2026 | judged rows | donor rows | A month absent | B month swapped in | gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 152,871 | 153,551 | 284.2948 s | 284.8740 s | **-0.5792 s** [-0.89, -0.29] |
| 7 | 191,792 | 190,440 | 216.9878 s | 216.4674 s | **+0.5203 s** [+0.22, +0.84] |

The same month a year earlier is **worth nothing**. The two months do not agree even on the sign and both moves are under a second,
where the within-year version was worth several. A year later the same calendar position carries none of it, which is the shape of
an effect that is fleet, schedule and airfield state rather than season. Taxi-in
is not taxi-out and `docs/YR.md` measured their month-to-month deviations as uncorrelated, so this is a proxy and is stated as one.
It is the only cross-year measurement the data admits, and it points one way.

## 5. What it decides

Two corrections were unpriced and they point opposite ways, so both had to be measured before either could be assumed.

The row mix is real and it has two terms. The ranking set's lane split is worth 1.3385 s of composite in our
favour and the lane-A cell mix takes 0.8202 s back, leaving 0.5183 s in our
favour before lane B. The lane-A term is the new one: it survives dropping the worst hundred rows, so unlike lane B's it is a
property of the row set rather than of a few outliers, and no projection this campaign has made carries it.

The training months are not a hidden credit. The board file sees January and July 2025 and no holdout fit does, which looked like an
advantage the holdout could not show; section 2 priced a month's own rows at several seconds and section 4 shows that value does not
cross a year. So the extra months buy about nothing on 2026 rows, and the holdout is a fair proxy on this axis rather than a
pessimistic one.

Together: the honest board projection is the holdout composite plus the mix shift, with no credit for the extra training months. It
does not change a champion, a cell or a file. It changes what the board numbers should be expected to be, and `docs/YR.md`'s
year-shift risk sits on top of it with a sign nothing here can settle. Flag F26.
