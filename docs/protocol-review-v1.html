Taxi-Out Protocol Review

Verdict: the protocol is sound in shape and wrong in three load-bearing places. The all-row holdout RMSE is dominated by 1.6% of rows that have no AOBT_3, the label leaks the truth into a declared feature, and the site admits open external data where the protocol says it is silent. Seventeen revisions and three decisions follow. Nothing runs until Keith says "apply consolidated".

  • 56% of the all-row holdout squared error sits in the 5,373 rows without AOBT_3 (RMSE 3,951 s there, 440 s elsewhere). Verdict lanes must split by AOBT_3 presence.
  • The board leader at 249.46 s cannot carry a 2025-like tail. If the scored rows held the same no-AOBT_3 structure, those rows alone would floor the RMSE at 494 s. Either 2026 lacks the tail or the scorer drops it. Inference, arithmetic below.
  • Codex (gpt-5.6-sol, xhigh) returned 12 findings, 8 overlapping the in-house pass. Its four unique ones are folded into R9, R10 and D1.

Decisions for Keith

D1 · Probe the scored subset through the board

The unknown 215,876-row subset defines the objective. The no-AOBT_3 group alone decides about 7.7 s of board RMSE depending on whether its rows are scored: 5,290 rows with a 400 s prediction offset add 3,920 s² to a 62,230 s² base. Two submissions per group, one with +c and one with -c on that group only, give its scored fraction and mean residual. Groups: AOBT_3 missing, January, July. About six submissions, each a logged ledger row.

Site: ranking.html says teams are ranked on the best RMSE across their submissions, so probes cannot lower the rank; no cap is stated anywhere. My call: yes, right after S1. Submissions are outward-facing, so this stays a Keith decision either way. Source: codex 9.

D2 · Nulls: analytic bound or skill-literal replays

A. Drop the 5-permutation nulls. Record per lane the expected best-of-grid optimism under no effect (12 cells: 1.63 x the paired SE, about 1.7 s, inside the 2 s floor); pick the grid by nested month-block CV; count every holdout verdict in the trial ledger. B. The skill as written: at least 20 full-recipe replays, 12 cells x 5 folds x 20 = 1,200 fits per lane.

My call: A. It deviates from the skill's process-null principle, which is why it is not mine to take. Source: codex 6, in-house.

D3 · External open data

eligibility.html: "All additional datasets used are openly available under an open source license" and must be documented. The protocol's data register says the site is silent; that is wrong (R15). A. Declare a weather lane W1 after S1 lands: an open METAR archive, its own register row and point-in-time rule, gated like any new model class. B. Keep external data foreclosed for this campaign.

My call: A, after S1. January tails are the likeliest place weather shows, and the rule allows it.

Codex outside voice, verbatim

codex exec 0.144.1, model gpt-5.6-sol, reasoning xhigh, read-only, no tools, 18,874 tokens. Prompt: the protocol v1 text with a header asking for at most 12 findings the in-house pass missed.

12 findings
  1. §§0, 4 (B4/M1) — “AOBT_3 residual” is defined as takeoff − AOBT_3 − truth, then appears as an M1 feature and ambiguously in B4. That is either target leakage or an unreproducible definition. Fix: define z = takeoff − AOBT_3 as the feature and c = target − z as the training label; enforce feature-lineage tests excluding c. Confidence: 10/10.
  2. §§0, 3, 4 — Jan/Jul has already been mined for the 600-second threshold, feature cap, baseline performance, and decision floor. “Holdout touched once” is false before M1 starts. Fix: relabel it development validation; use nested date-block CV and seal untouched dates or months for final auditing. Confidence: 10/10.
  3. §3 — The Jan/Jul holdout is fitted using later 2025 months, while deployment predicts 2026 using only the prior year. This validates temporal interpolation, not forward transfer, and combining January with July can hide opposite failures. Fix: add rolling-origin folds and report January and July separately. Confidence: 9/10.
  4. §0 — The claimed 620 independent units ignore same-date cross-airport shocks and adjacent-day persistence. The n_independent/8 feature cap is an unjustified linear-model heuristic based on holdout rather than training information. Fix: use date-level moving blocks or two-way clustering and control LightGBM complexity through grouped OOF results, not a 77-feature cap. Confidence: 9/10.
  5. §§0, 3 — Resampling to the board’s row count does not estimate uncertainty for the actual holdout estimator. A paired SE observed for one 19.4-second contrast cannot establish a universal 2-second noise floor; paired variance changes by challenger. Fix: bootstrap each exact paired comparison with at least 2,000 replicates and call 2 seconds a practical threshold, not detectability. Confidence: 10/10.
  6. §§3, 4 — Permuting raw targets within airport-days is not exchangeable given hour and queue structure, and it tests the wrong null relative to B3. Five permutations have minimum p-value 1/6 and provide a noisy maximum at enormous cost. Fix: use nested blocked validation, or residual randomization under an explicit null with at least 100 permutations only for finalists. Confidence: 10/10.
  7. §§3, 8 — Delta signs are inconsistent: “−6.3” appears favorable, “at least 2” implies positive is favorable, while negative board deltas trigger review. Stage 6 also requests paired board intervals despite having no scored-row truths. Fix: define improvement = old RMSE − new RMSE, positive is good, and report board point deltas only. Confidence: 10/10.
  8. §§1, 4 — Because target = takeoff − block and takeoff is visible, this is principally hidden off-block-time imputation. The protocol jumps to traffic and tail models before exhausting the visible timestamp estimates. Fix: test takeoff−EOBT, takeoff−LOBT, and takeoff−AOBT_3, followed by an OOF linear/median airport calibration of their implied block times. Confidence: 10/10.
  9. §§7, 8 — The unknown 215,876-row subset is not merely a caution flag; it defines the objective’s weighting distribution. Optimizing all-row holdout RMSE may target the wrong population. Fix: first reconcile submission-template IDs, then—if rules permit—use ± perturbation submissions by month, airport, and missingness group to infer scored fractions and group residual sums. Confidence: 9/10.
  10. §§3, 8 — The board cannot remain an independent judge after unlimited adaptive submissions; the ledger and haircut do not remove winner’s curse. Arbitrary waves of three also reduce the chance of top three. Fix: retain a sealed local audit for scientific claims, while explicitly budgeting board probes and low-dimensional calibration/blend searches, logging every query. Confidence: 10/10.
  11. §§1, 7 — Preventing Huber or transformed-target fits from “flipping” the verdict confuses evaluation loss with the training estimator. Labels near 131,000 seconds can destabilize MSE training, while robust training may still improve raw-RMSE evaluation. Fix: admit clipping, winsorized, and Huber candidates, judging all of them solely on raw grouped-validation RMSE. Confidence: 10/10.
  12. §4 (M3) — Ten large per-airport TabPFN fits, 77 features, 345,000-row inference, weight searches, and permutation replays are not established as feasible on 16 GB VRAM. The advertised one-million-row limit is not a runtime or memory benchmark. Fix: run one representative-airport pilot with hard wall-clock, VRAM, and ≥2-second OOF-gain gates before allocating the full lane. Confidence: 9/10.
