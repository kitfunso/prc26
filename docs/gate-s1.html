The rule, restated

  • Improvement = champion RMSE minus challenger RMSE on the same holdout rows, per lane. Lane A decides; lane B is judged on its own rows; the all-row number never decides.
  • Verdict statistic: paired day-block bootstrap of the improvement, 2,000 reps, seed 0, whole days resampled, 62 days. WIN needs the 95% interval above zero and a point improvement of at least 2 s. A win with opposite signs in January and July is a flag.
  • Every search declares k and pays an analytic optimism bound of E[max of k standard normals] x paired SE. A gain must clear bound plus floor.
  • Ties break on parsimony: fewer features, simpler class, fewer stages.
  • Board RMSE on the 215,876 scored rows is exact for the ranking and a point value for learning; haircut = holdout improvement minus board improvement, one row per submission. REVIEW fires when two promoted champions in a row show board improvement at or below zero.

This verdict is only as good as this rule. Attack the rule, not just the numbers.

Self-audit (Stage 5.6): what else is wrong with what I did

  1. Data. The six traffic counts see only the loaded period, so windows at the first and last 15 minutes of a loaded file undercount. Holdout and ranking are both a Jan+Jul pair loaded together, so the edge shape matches at scoring time, but a January-31 window never sees February in either. Codes unseen in training: ranking versus all 12 months has 0.08% unseen stands and 0.01% unseen aircraft types, zero for runway, wake and segment; LightGBM recodes them to missing (its pandas path calls set_categories on the training list). Ranking nulls: actype 0.11%, wake 1.54%, segment 1.53%. The traffic counts were checked against a brute-force recount on 300 sampled February departures (tests/test_traffic.py, passes).
  2. Statistics. Both lanes ride 62 day blocks, whatever their row counts. The lane-B interval spans 630 s because a handful of days carry its squared error; the lane-A interval is 6.7 s wide. The grid's parsimony window was applied to point CV RMSEs (cells 1, 5 and 9 sit within 1.3 s of each other) with no interval; the choice among them cannot matter by more than the floor. The final round count is the mean best iteration across five folds trained on eight months, then applied to ten months and, for S1, twelve; more rows usually want more rounds, so the fixed count errs on the under-fit side.
  3. Code. The knockout mode was smoke-tested on lane B only before the lane-A run; its lane-A output is the table above; the run's full-model line, 232.9 s at 1,016 rounds, matches the CV grid's chosen cell. The same predict path serves CV, holdout, forward fold and S1, so a bug there moves every number the same way rather than one of them. No upload code exists yet; it is written after the yes and its checksum goes in the ledger row.
  4. Process. The protocol declared "at most 12 cells" for M1 and the bound was computed at k = 12, but the grid axes (leaves, min_data_in_leaf, objective) were fixed at run time, not written down before it. The huber alpha of 600 s was sized for lane-A residuals and is mis-sized for lane-B targets in the thousands, so lane B had four live cells and four dead ones; its bound at k = 8 overstates the search, which is the conservative direction. The knockout was launched after the verdict, as declared. The same hands authored, ran and judged everything; the independent critique below is the outside voice.

Gate questions

The six questions from the steering bank, verbatim, each with my answer. The answers are mine to be attacked; the questions are Keith's to press.

1Attack the decision rule, not just the numbers.

The rule's blind spot is the board's scored subset (F1). If the scorer drops lane B, the lane-A verdict is the whole game and S1 reads cleanly. If it keeps lane B, half the squared error on the board comes from rows where we run a two-feature OLS (F9), and S1 says little about M1. Second, the bootstrap treats the 62 days of Jan+Jul 2025 as the population; 2026 is a fresh draw (F2, F5). Third, at 51.6 s against a 2.8 s bound the rule is not what decides here; transfer decides, and the rule cannot measure transfer until a file lands.

2Tied on WHAT?

Nothing is tied at the verdict level. Lane A cleared its bound about 18 times over; lane B lost on both point and interval. Inside the lane-A grid, cells 1, 5 and 9 (31, 127 and 255 leaves at min_data 200, l2) were tied within the 2 s floor at 232.9, 231.6 and 231.8 s; parsimony picked 31 leaves. Rounds were not on the grid; early stopping set them.

3What market structure could explain or refute this?

Here "market" is the airport. Stand carries 11% and runway 14% of the lane-A gain, so a stand renumbering or a runway configuration change in 2026 removes what the model learned. The 2025 tail (F3) came from somewhere physical: a diversion day, a snow day, a ground stop; I do not know which, and whether 2026 has its own. UNKNOWN: any 2026 event at the ten airports. Nothing in the data names one.

4Is the capacity right for the data frequency?

Lane A: 2.06 million rows, 20 features, 31 leaves, 1,016 rounds. CV picked the smallest tree within the floor, and December (223.2 s) came in below the CV level, so nothing says over-fit at the month scale. Lane B: 17,097 rows and a tail measured in hours; the tree class lost to two-feature OLS on holdout and forward fold alike. Yes for lane A, no for lane B, which is why the lanes carry different classes.

5What would make you say no?

The four conditions in the section above. None fired: the critique found no leak and its one condition (finding 1) is met, the tests pass, the file builds against the template, and the levels track the inputs. The fourth condition is Keith's to fire.

6What is on the accrual list, and when is each re-test?

The accrual list is empty. No verdict rode fewer than 300 flights (protocol section 6; the smallest per-airport lane holds about 22,000). Scheduled after S1, in order: probes P1-P6 (six uploads, derive the scored count and error sum per group), W1 register row, M2, M3 one-airport pilot, E1. Each new class gates on Keith before its first upload.
