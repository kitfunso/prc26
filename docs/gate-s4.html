Two consequences. First, the split itself is unmeasurable: a submission returns sum(p'² − p²) − 2 sum((p' − p) y) with both prediction vectors known, so every probe returns one linear functional of the truth vector, while a lane's SSE is quadratic in it; the four unknowns against the four available files are rank three. P1 to P6's first moments were the most the leaderboard could ever have given on this question, and the remaining scorer slots buy nothing more of it. Second, v16's per-lane board error sums are exact with no upload, carried from S1's measured ones by the difference of the two files on disk: +5.59 s per lane-A row and −208.6 s per lane-B row. Removing the lane-B level alone is worth about 1.17 s of board composite at no model cost, and it is a change to a submitted file, so it is question 6 below.

Put v16 on the board at the equal-ratio point (lane A 205.8, lane B 1,618, composite 286.12) and the leader at 265.7612 must be 15.1% better than us in lane B if their lane A matches ours, or 14.5% better in lane A if their lane B matches ours. 7. Caution flags, all of them

  • F18, new and open. The 2 s promotion floor is shared by both lanes and means very different things in each. Lane A's paired standard error is about 0.2 s, so the floor is roughly 10 of them, and it has killed four challengers whose intervals sat entirely above zero, worth about 2.7 s together. Lane B's paired standard error is 16.6 s, so the floor is about an eighth of one and gates almost nothing. Partly answered 2026-09-07, and not by changing the rule. SB3 composed the four parked challengers on D2's base and cleared the floor at +2.06 s [+1.71, +2.37] (H52), so the floor delayed those gains rather than discarding them. That does not settle the flag, because the asymmetry between the lanes is unchanged and lane B's floor still gates almost nothing, but it removes the strongest piece of evidence for lowering it in lane A. Changing this is a decision-rule change and yours to make, so nothing was altered.
  • F17, restated. Nine of 344,419 holdout rows carry 34.4% of the squared error and two carry 28.4%. It licenses one thing only: our own board score is a high-variance draw from the row sets we might have faced. It licenses nothing about a difference measured on the fixed set, so a negative board move between two of our own files is a real regression and the REVIEW tripwire still fires on it.
  • F19, new and open. The lane-A baseline moved 27.4 s under the A1 anchor guard and no earlier report says so. A1 was re-verdicted against the champions only (H29 to H32, all ties). 8. The decision rule, restated

A challenger replaces a champion only if the paired day-block bootstrap improvement on the untouched holdout has its whole interval above zero, and its point estimate clears a 2 s floor plus an optimism bound priced at the lane's declared cell count k. Ties promote the simpler model. A search not declared in protocol section 4 before it ran is a diagnostic and can never decide a verdict.

This verdict is only as good as this rule. Attack the rule, not just the numbers.

F18 is the sharpest attack anyone has made on it so far: one floor, two lanes, and an eighty-fold difference in what it costs to pass.
