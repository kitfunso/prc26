7. Caution flags, all of them

  • F18, new and open. The 2 s promotion floor is shared by both lanes and means very different things in each. Lane A's paired standard error is about 0.2 s, so the floor is roughly 10 of them, and it has killed four challengers whose intervals sat entirely above zero, worth about 2.7 s together. Lane B's paired standard error is 16.6 s, so the floor is about an eighth of one and gates almost nothing. Since MC2, lane A has promoted nothing in ten attempts and lane B has promoted three times. Changing this is a decision-rule change and yours to make, so nothing was altered.
  • F17, restated. Nine of 344,419 holdout rows carry 34.4% of the squared error and two carry 28.4%. It licenses one thing only: our own board score is a high-variance draw from the row sets we might have faced. It licenses nothing about a difference measured on the fixed set, so a negative board move between two of our own files is a real regression and the REVIEW tripwire still fires on it.
  • F19, new and open. The lane-A baseline moved 27.4 s under the A1 anchor guard and no earlier report says so. A1 was re-verdicted against the champions only (H29 to H32, all ties). Refit on the restored pre-A1 anchor columns, B4 scores 306.8 s floored against 334.2 s today, on 97 changed anchor values and 4,437 changed pushback anchors out of 339,046 rows. The guard stays, because F11 says an anchor after takeoff cannot be known at prediction time. The reporting consequence is that the ledger's B4 lane-A figure of 311.2 belongs to neither fit, and lane-A gains quoted against B4 on today's data carry about 27 s of baseline shift inside them. CA1 quotes both.
  • F20, new and open. The ranking set's lane-B rows sit in different cells from the holdout's, and the shift goes against us. The lane split transfers (1.534% against 1.560%) but lane B moved from July-heavy to balanced, and the January cells that grew are the ones the champion handles worst. The honest size, after the two LFPG rows F17 names are dropped, is +13 to +39 s of lane-B RMSE and about +1 to +3 s of composite, which makes both board anchors in section 3 slightly optimistic. It corrects a projection; it licenses no change to a model. Measured in docs/MIX.md.
  • F21, new and closed on arrival. The largest single gain in the campaign is a recording artifact and nothing reaches it. A lane-B taxi time within an hour of a full day is a block time written one day early, so the truth is 86,400 s plus an ordinary taxi. Six such holdout rows exist; predicting them is worth +510.7 s of lane B, about 45 s of composite, which is more than our whole gap to the leader. It closes three ways: +399.2 s of that sits on one LFPG row with an ordinary 1,740 s schedule and no mark on it; the five marked rows share their mark (a schedule past 40,000 s) with 12 ordinary flights and 8 schedule copies in the same 25-row population, so the cell died before it was declared; and both blind answers lose, a uniform shift at -0.2 [-2.0, +1.1] and the two-value split at -64.9 [-173.1, +10.0]. The 2026 arrivals carry the artifact 0 times in 344,693 rows. The consequence for question (d) below: a board gap of a few tens of seconds is not by itself proof that a better recipe exists. Measured in docs/DE.md.
  • F22, new and open. Lane A's tail is variance, not bias, and two model families miss on the same rows. The top 1% of lane-A rows hold 36.1% of the lane's squared error and 83.3% of that is under-prediction, which looks like a model that shrinks long taxis. Conditioned on the prediction it is calibrated (decile bias -12.5 to +4.1 s); the table conditioned on the truth shows +110.4 to -275.8 s and is regression to the mean, not shrinkage. The ceiling on any additive recalibration is +0.04 s by predicted decile and +0.42 s by airport and runway, so calibration and tail expansion close by arithmetic. MC2 and D1 are different families on the same 49 columns: their errors correlate 0.960 in the tail, their worst-1% row sets overlap 78%, their mean is worse than the better one, and a row-by-row oracle over the two reaches only 214.3 s. 8. The decision rule, restated

A challenger replaces a champion only if the paired day-block bootstrap improvement on the untouched holdout has its whole interval above zero, and its point estimate clears a 2 s floor plus an optimism bound priced at the lane's declared cell count k. Ties promote the simpler model. A search not declared in protocol section 4 before it ran is a diagnostic and can never decide a verdict.

This verdict is only as good as this rule. Attack the rule, not just the numbers.

F18 is the sharpest attack anyone has made on it so far: one floor, two lanes, and an eighty-fold difference in what it costs to pass.

9. Independent Stage 5.7 critique, verbatim

Protocol Stage 5.7 requires a reviewer that did not build or run the campaign to attack the method, given artifacts rather than summaries, with its findings riding the gate surface verbatim. This one read the protocol, the ledger, TAIL, APT, FORWARD, L8, L9, COVERAGE, LB-CONC and the source of verdict.py, champ.py, l6.py and l8.py, and re-ran the arithmetic itself. It is reproduced in full and unedited. Findings 1, 4 and 5 have since been measured, and findings 6, 9, 10 and 13 have been corrected in the documents; nothing below has been softened to match.

1. The chain bound is never computed, only asserted per link. Lane B has had 10 holdout contacts since B4-floored (ledger H23, H24, H25, H26, H27, H32, H33, H42, H43, H44) across ~25 declared cells, and 3 promotions — yet no verdict of L8c against B4-floored exists anywhere. Each link priced its own k (H43: "k = 4 bound 17.1 s"). Under one bound for the whole lane, E[max of 25] ≈ 1.97 and the root-sum-square of the three promotion paired SEs (12.8, 23.6, 16.6 from H25/H27/H43) is 31.6 s → bound ≈ 62 s against a claimed chain total of +157.4 s (1965.6 → 1808.2). So multiplicity does not overturn the point estimate — but the interval that would settle it was never run. SERIOUS Fix: one paired_delta(err_B4floored, err_L8c, days) call on cached errors; record it as H45.

2. The 2 s floor is shared across lanes and is 80x looser in lane B. taxi/verdict.py:10 FLOOR = 2.0, used by Verdict.win (line 20) for both lanes. Lane A paired SE ≈ 0.2 s (H28, H38, H40) → the floor is ~10 SE. Lane B paired SE 16.6 s (H43) → the floor is 0.12 SE. Consequence, visible in the ledger: since MC2, lane A has had ten challengers and zero promotions, four of them with intervals strictly above zero killed by the floor alone (H21 +0.498 [+0.186,+0.909], H28 +1.0 [+0.5,+1.5], H34 +0.2 [+0.1,+0.3], H35 +1.0 [+0.7,+1.3], ≈2.7 s discarded); lane B, over the same window, promoted three times. The campaign's recent gains land in lane B partly because the gate there barely exists. SERIOUS Fix: FLOOR = max(2.0, 2 * paired_SE) per contrast.

3. Most of the headline improvement is 1.56% of the rows. Composite from the ledger's own lane RMSEs: S1 = 365.24 (row S1: 259.5 / 2074.1 on 339,046 / 5,373), S4 = 322.58. Holding lane B at S1 and moving only lane A gives 346.65 (−18.6 s); holding lane A and moving only lane B gives 342.48 (−22.8 s). 53% of the 42.7 s gain comes from 5,373 lane-B rows whose level bootstrap SD is 400.57 s (docs/TAIL.md, levels table). SERIOUS Fix: report this split on the gate page, not the composite alone.

4. L8c's win is not a few-row artifact, but its falsifier was skipped. The gain sits in bands 1-3, 347 rows (docs/L8.md, touched-rows table); band 3 alone (136 rows, 4600 → 3847) is ~82% of the SSE reduction; bands 4, 5, 6 are unchanged, so the TAIL extreme rows in those bands cannot be driving it.
